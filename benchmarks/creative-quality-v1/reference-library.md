# Reference library

## Collection policy

References establish a comparative quality bar. They are not templates to copy.

For every reference, record its visual system, focal object, asset roles, typography, depth treatment, likely production method, and the editing primitives required to reproduce the underlying class of composition.

## Early observations

- The campaign does not rely on one reusable headline-and-support stack.
- The real product packshot remains the visual source of truth.
- Typography changes scale, position, curvature, and role across concepts while remaining recognizably branded.
- Supporting assets carry meaning: comparison bars, ingredients, crushed packaging, lifestyle context, and illustration are not decoration alone.
- Depth comes from shadows, lighting, texture, overlap, and environmental surfaces.
- The visual idea precedes the layout. Layout is a consequence of the argument each poster makes.
- Calls to action and brand marks are finished deterministically and consistently.

## Coverage snapshot

- 30 references recorded: 16 commercial-tool outputs and 14 human-led works.
- Current strength: physical product, campaign systems, dimensional type, and information design.
- Remaining weakness: current software-interface launch advertising and source-led editorial work.
- Before blind review, add 8–12 references in those weak categories and locally record visual notes after inspection.

## Collection target

The minimum count is met, but breadth still matters. Before blind review, bring the library to 38–42 references:

- 5 software or interface-led launch posters.
- 4 evidence or primary-source-led editorial posters.
- 2 restrained B2B technical posters.

At least half of the final library should come from human-led brand or agency work rather than AI-tool showcases.
